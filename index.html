Goes with: Feature Engineering

What does a log transform do to a skewed feature?

300 simulated incomes with a long right tail. Switch the transform and watch the histogram and the skewness.

Skewness
Mean / median

Notice. Skewness near 0 means a roughly symmetric shape. The square root helps a little, log1p helps most for this kind of tail. Linear models and distance-based models usually behave better on the transformed version.

Goes with: Building Models

Underfitting and overfitting, with one slider

A k-nearest-neighbours model on two noisy classes. The background colour is what the model would predict at that spot. k is how many neighbours get a vote.

class Aclass Bfilled = training, ring = test
Train accuracy
Test accuracy

Notice. At k = 1 the model memorises every training point: 100% on training data, worse on test data. That is overfitting. At a very large k the boundary is too smooth and both scores drop. That is underfitting. The best k sits in between, and you only find it by scoring on data the model has not seen.

Goes with: Model Selection and Evaluation

Move the threshold, change the mistakes

Each dot is one of the 114 test tumours, placed at the probability our logistic regression gave it of being malignant. Anything right of the line is predicted malignant.

Notice. Slide left and recall rises, because fewer cancers are missed, but more healthy patients get a false alarm, so precision falls. Slide right and the reverse happens. Accuracy hardly shows this trade. Which mistake is worse is a decision about the problem, and the threshold is where you make it.

Goes with: Feature Importance

Two ways to ask which features matter

The ten highest-ranked features of our random forest, scored two ways. Click a bar to see what shuffling that column does to the test F1 of 0.950.

Click a bar.

Notice. Built-in importance ranks worst radius and mean radius highly, yet shuffling them changes nothing. Radius, perimeter and area measure the same thing (correlation 0.99), so when one is scrambled the forest reads the others. Importance tells you what this model leans on. It does not tell you what causes cancer.

Goes with: Model Explainability with LIME

How LIME explains one prediction

The coloured background is a black-box model with a curved boundary. Click anywhere to choose the case to explain. LIME scatters fake neighbours around it, asks the black box about each one, and fits a straight line that only has to be right nearby.

large dots = close neighbours, count moredashed line = LIME's local model

Notice. Move the case along the boundary and the explanation changes, because the curve points a different way there. Widen the neighbourhood and the dashed line stops matching the curve near your case. A LIME explanation is local: it describes one prediction, never the whole model.
